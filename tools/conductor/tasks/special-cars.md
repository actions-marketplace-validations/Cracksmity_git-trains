Draw three special cars in `src/trains/special-cars.js`: a **dining car**, an **observation car** and a **mail car**. They join the train in the scene when the GitHub profile earns them, so they should look like prized, distinct pieces of the same train.

Context: `src/trains/render.js` builds a train as pixel layers (a `Grid` per layer, see `src/graphics/pixel.js`), facing right. Each car is 64 px wide (`MID_W`) and the body is 20 rows tall (`BODY_ROWS`), with 9 free rows above the roof (`TOP`) for the pantograph and sparks. `buildTrain(model, { cars, extra })` now accepts `extra: 'dining' | 'observation' | 'mail'`: one more car right behind the cab. For that car, `drawCar` already paints the silhouette (rows 0-1 roof highlight, 2 roof, 3 roof edge, 4-10 upper body, 11-17 bands, 18-19 skirt), the gangway at the left end, the automatic outline, the bogies and the equipment boxes. Then it calls `paintSpecialCar(kind, car)` in `src/trains/special-cars.js`, which has three **placeholder** painters (`dining`, `observation`, `mail`) that you replace with real art. Read `src/trains/special-cars.js` first: the contract (the `car` fields, the coordinates, the `put` and `rect` helpers that never leave the car's own columns) is in its header comment. Read `drawCar` in `src/trains/render.js` to see how the ordinary cars paint their windows (frame in `pal.outline`, glass in `pal.glass`, a `pal.glassHi` highlight, `ctx.panes` for the windows that light up with the streak, the `lit` layer in `#ffd86b`), doors and sign, and `src/trains/models.js` for the palettes: **a special car must look like it belongs to whichever of the six models it joins** (orange, gray, yellow and white metros, the tram, the bullet train), and to custom trains too, so paint from `car.model.pal` (and `car.stripe`), never from fixed colors.

The three cars (the user's brief, in their words): a **dining car** (round windows, a little lamp) for a 100-day streak, an **observation car** (glass roof) for 100+ stars, a **mail car** (no windows, a stripe) for 500+ pull requests. What good looks like:

- **Dining car**: a row of round portholes (about 6-7 px across, ringed in `pal.outline`, glass with a highlight pixel or two) instead of the rectangular windows, evenly spaced, with warm light inside them on the `lit` layer so they glow, plus a little lamp: for example a small lantern on the roof or a wall lamp between two portholes, glowing on the `lit` layer. Keep the usual body bands; maybe a trim stripe that echoes the ordinary cars.
- **Observation car**: the roof is glass. Raise a glass canopy above the roof line (rows above 0 are free, up to about 4 rows is plenty): a trapezoid or gently rounded dome of `pal.glass` with `pal.glassHi` highlights and a few outline ribs, running most of the car's length, with the roof's own rows under it showing glass too. Big panoramic side windows below it. No pantograph.
- **Mail car**: no windows at all and no glass anywhere on the side. A sliding cargo door with a handle, a wide stripe in `car.stripe` (the language color) along the bands, a small envelope or post-horn emblem in the trim color, a few roof vents. Plain and sturdy.

All three must be clearly different from an ordinary car and from each other at a glance, at the real size (the scene is shown at 2x, so each pixel is 2x2 on screen), and good on every model. Pixel art: few colors, clean shapes, hard edges, no noise or dithering.

How to see your work: `node scripts/sheet.js .scratch/cars.png 4 3 0 metro-orange,metro-white,bullet,tram a dining` writes a zoomed PNG of 3-car trains with the special car added (the last argument is `dining`, `observation` or `mail`; use `a` for the wheel frame). `.scratch/` is git-ignored. Look at the PNG (and at the other models: `node -e "import('./src/trains/models.js').then(m=>console.log(m.MODEL_ORDER.join(',')))"`) and iterate until it looks right. Also try one steam train (`examples/trains/steam.json`, where the car sits right behind the locomotive) and two cars (`2` as the cars argument).

Hard rules:

- Only edit `src/trains/special-cars.js`. Keep the exports `SPECIAL_CARS` and `paintSpecialCar(kind, car)` and its header comment accurate (update the comment where your art changes what it says). Do not touch `src/trains/render.js`.
- Colors: from `car.model.pal`, `car.stripe`, and blends of those with `mix` (`import { mix } from './train.js'`: the import is circular with train.js and that is fine because `mix` is only called when a car is drawn). The only fixed hex colors allowed are the warm light ones the train already uses: `#ffd86b`, `#f2d98d`, `#ffb02e`, `#fff4b0`.
- Paint with `car.put` and `car.rect` so the car never spills into its neighbours. Do not paint the bogies, the equipment boxes below row 17 or the gangway at the left edge: `drawCar` does that.
- The tests in `tests/activity/unlocks.test.js` are the contract. They check, among other things, that the observation car has at least 40 glass pixels (`pal.glass` or `pal.glassHi`) on the roof band, that the mail car has none in rows 3-17 and at least 20 pixels of the stripe color, and that the dining car has at least 3 glass windows that are round (the corners of each window's bounding box are not glass) and something on the `lit` layer. Do not edit the tests. If a test contradicts a good design, say so in your final message instead of editing it.
- Tests: `npm test` must pass.
